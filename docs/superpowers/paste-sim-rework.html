foreman · terminal emulation · design brief

Replace the CUP scanner with paste simulation

Foreman already knows the exact pasted string and the pre-paste cursor. Instead of intercepting PSReadLine's escape sequences with a second VT parser, predict where PSReadLine will park the cursor — and where it should have parked it — with pure arithmetic, then alias only on an exact match.

The bug being healed

PSReadLine 2.4.5 measures a non-BMP emoji as two width-1 UTF-16 units (upstream #1329).

When a pasted emoji reaches a row margin, the real renderer defers the whole width-2 glyph to the next row; PSReadLine's coordinate math splits it 1+1 across the boundary instead. Every such split leaves its final cursor-position command (CUP) one cell behind reality. The text is fine — the caret is wrong.

PS> paste 🥒🤣🤣🤣 🥒🤣🤣🤣 🥒🤣🤣🤣 … (wraps 21 rows)
…🤣🤣🤣 🥒🤣🤣🤣 🥒🤣      ← caret paints here; text ends 4 cells later
covered today

Pad variant

Short wrapped pastes echo back as soft-wrapped rows with LEADING_WIDE_CHAR_SPACER pads and WRAPLINE flags. The scanner counts non-BMP pads over the wrap chain and aliases when the CUP shortfall matches exactly.

uncovered — the reason for this rework

Hard-positioned variant

Large multi-row pastes (10+ rows) come back from ConPTY as hard-positioned, fully-packed rows — zero pads, zero WRAPLINE flags. The pad-count gate compares a shortfall of N against 0 expected pads and fails closed; the caret sits N cells behind (measured: 4 cells over a 21-row paste).

How it works

Two pure simulations at paste time, an O(1)-gated check per PTY chunk.

At paste time the arming gates stay exactly as shipped (PowerShell, primary screen, single line, cursor at content end — plus new fail-closed gates for control characters and wrap-pending endpoints). If armed, foreman runs the pasted string through two width models from the recorded pre-paste cursor:

P — PSReadLine's math

Per UTF-16 unit. A non-BMP emoji is two independent width-1 units that can split at a row margin. P predicts exactly where PSReadLine's final CUP will park the raw cursor.

P ≠ E ?
E — whole-glyph flow

Per char, widths from unicode-width 0.2.2 — the same single crate version alacritty parses with, so E is byte-identical to what the grid will do. E is where the caret truly belongs.

If P == E (no emoji straddles a margin) nothing arms — zero ongoing cost. Otherwise, after each pumped chunk, a scroll-invariant check (amended twice under review — no absolute rows, no scroll bookkeeping): an O(1) column compare gates everything — the raw cursor's column must equal P's; only then do the bounded row-walks run: the raw cursor must sit strictly behind content (!cursor_at_content_end(raw)), and the point E′ = raw advanced by the precomputed E − P cell delta must satisfy the v3 fingerprint (settled by Phase 0's byte-at-a-time scans): (a) every cell from E′ to the end of the viewport is empty, and (b) walking back from E′, the grid matches the full expected E-layout of the pasted text — char, wide-base, and spacer/pad identity, all of it. Weaker forms are measured-dead: "raw strictly behind content" loses coverage when raw lands on a trailing-space cell (blind holdout, delta = 1), and any bounded-length local tail fingerprint accepts wrong rows on periodic payloads whose rep divides the width (9 wrong aliases in the holdout scan). Full extent is the only check that survived both captures with zero wrong aliases across ~33k possible chunk boundaries. All true → alias raw → E′, re-verified on every subsequent chunk; a failed re-verification ends the epoch permanently — no later re-acceptance. The caret paints at E′, snapshot --cursor reports it, and the very next Backspace seeds the wide-key shadow from it immediately — no 50 ms settle wait, same as today.

Epoch lifetime tightens versus today: any user-sent bytes end the epoch (today only CR/LF/^C do). This replaces the scanner's per-byte echo-print invalidation at its cause — the pinned type-then-Backspace case dies with the keystroke instead of with its echo — while the first post-paste Backspace still consumes the alias to seed the shadow in the same frame before its bytes go out. One residual is accepted and documented: child-originated output after acceptance that keeps the cursor on P's column and preserves the tail fingerprint survives re-verification (today's per-byte print detection would have dropped it). The stricter variant — any post-acceptance output ends the epoch — is a spec decision deferred until Phase 0's replay shows how often ConPTY delivers trailing fragments after the final CUP.

The exact-match on P is the safety story: width behavior the model doesn't capture — ZWJ sequences, exotic prompts, apps that merely look like PSReadLine — lands the cursor off P's column and the alias never engages. The stated residual: a geometric collision needs the cursor at P's exact column and content ending at exactly raw+(E−P) inside an armed epoch. Wrong predictions cost coverage; correctness requires that double coincidence.

What the alias feeds — unchanged

The three consumers are untouched: caret paint (display_cursor_point), foreman snapshot --cursor (effective_cursor_info), and wide-key shadow seeding (input_cursor_point + has_fresh_cursor_alias). Alacritty's grid, CPR replies, and VT parsing stay authoritative. All of WideShadowState / WideKeyLatch / keep_wide_shadow stays — hold-repeat is a different problem, and it remains solved.

Evidence gates before "fixed"

  • Phase 0 — frozen protocol, then probe (blocks everything below): no product code. A short immutable protocol doc fixes: exact build, prompt, dimensions, codepoints, coordinate types (flat cell offsets from pre-paste start), predicted P, E, delta, settle rule, and three scenarios — primary (21-row repro), a frozen blind holdout (different cols/prompt/emoji mix — defined now, its output never inspected while refining P; a holdout-informed revision doesn't count as validation), and a P==E control. The probe carries an independent actual_end oracle (full-viewport scan from the bottom, not the production predicate evaluated twice) plus the expected tail fingerprint; raw < actual_end < E′ is pre-registered as fail-closed only as verified by that oracle — stale residue at E′−1 can defeat the predicate alone (fourth review). Instrument: a release-mode #[ignore]d harness that captures RX as typed, length-framed records (raw rx_dump concatenates chunks unframed and interleaves replies/resizes — unusable for replay as-is), logs each CUP's byte offset with pre/post cursor, then replays the stream byte-at-a-time through a VoidListener term, evaluating the acceptance predicate after every byte — every possible chunk boundary, not just the ones one run happened to observe. The CLI snapshot loop is a secondary black-box check only (--cursor reports the effective cursor and can't see the internals). Kill-switch: settled raw ≠ P → refine P against the primary's bytes and grade the revision on the untouched holdout, or park the rework with the negative result recorded. A green Phase 0 establishes feasibility on the frozen fixtures — not global safety, and not yet permission to delete the scanner.
  • Pure tests: P/E arithmetic tables (both policies, both variants' fixtures), acceptance + invalidation transitions, wrap-pending and control-char fail-closed gates, one ZWJ-sequence fixture pinning the fail-closed outcome for widths PSReadLine models differently, and a chunk-boundary-exactly-at-P regression pinning the third acceptance condition.
  • Live tests (ignored, real ConPTY): the existing live_psreadline_paste_wrap_uses_the_whole_emoji_endpoint must pass unchanged; a new hard-positioned multi-row test pastes 10+ wrapped rows of 🥒🤣🤣🤣 and asserts caret == content end + first Backspace doubles.
  • Headless probes: the handoff's foreman snapshot --cursor / --attrs loop against a release build, both variants, before any claim.
  • Perf guard: scanner_overhead_on_plain_and_ansi_floods keeps pinning the unarmed path, plus a new benchmark for the armed path (column-gated walks under a flood) — the existing test never measured it.

Prior art — how everyone else handles this

The bug is not foreman's: it reproduces in Windows Terminal (#529) and VS Code (PSReadLine #2358). Upstream PSReadLine #1329 has been open since 2019.

TechniqueWho applies itWhy it doesn't close this bug for foreman
Fix the line editor — PSReadLine #1329: LengthInBufferCells doesn't combine surrogate pairsupstream The right layer, stalled ~7 years. Foreman already ships Microsoft's newest ConPTY; a package bump can't repair it. Waiting is not a plan.
Width negotiation — DEC mode 2027 / Terminal Unicode Core (Contour spec; WezTerm, foot, kitty, WT 1.22 all discuss/ship it)terminal + app Solves multi-codepoint grapheme disagreement between app and terminal — but only for apps that opt in. PSReadLine doesn't, and its bug is self-inconsistency (its cursor math disagrees with its own output), not a table mismatch with us.
Compatibility measurement mode — WT 1.22 compatibility.textMeasurement: console measures like the vintage consoleterminal, globally The closest cousin to our design: model the legacy math so old apps stay self-consistent. But it's global — it would degrade every modern TUI (Claude Code, lazygit) to heal one paste case, and alacritty's width tables aren't pluggable anyway.
App-side probing & correction tables — fish/ucs-detect-style CPR probing, wcstwidth per-terminal correction tablesapp / line editor The app measures reality instead of assuming it. Effective — and entirely in the app's hands. PSReadLine doesn't probe.
Render faithfully, caret be damned — Windows Terminal default, VS Code/xterm.jsterminal The mainstream answer: humans shrug and press Ctrl+L. Foreman can't — agents read snapshot --cursor headlessly and wide-key doubling needs the true cursor; a lying caret misleads machines, not just eyes.

The dual-simulation is best understood as a per-epoch, fail-closed version of WT's console compatibility mode: we model PSReadLine's legacy math (P) exactly as that setting does — but only to recognize the known bug's fingerprint for the duration of one paste echo, while continuing to render modern widths (E) everywhere. Global degradation is the cost WT pays; the epoch's gates are the cost we pay.
